## Executive Summary

This document synthesizes proven cold calling frameworks from five foundational sales books into an actionable playbook tailored for Landline's B2B manufacturing sales platform. It covers activity-based prospecting, pre-call research methodology, question-based openers, multi-channel orchestration, and practical guidance on timing, gatekeepers, voicemail, and objection handling.

## Part 1: Core Frameworks from Leading Sales Methodologies

### 1.1 Fanatical Prospecting — Jeb Blount

Jeb Blount's central thesis is uncompromising: the root cause of an empty pipeline is the failure to consistently prospect. Prospecting is not an activity you "fit in" — it is the engine of revenue. His framework rests on controllable behaviors rather than motivation, with discipline beating inspiration every time.

**The Prospecting Pyramid.** Blount's strategic framework prioritizes leads based on their likelihood to convert. Salespeople segment prospects into tiers — from cold leads at the base to hot opportunities at the top — and allocate time proportionally. For manufacturing sales, this means spending 60% of calling time on warm leads (companies that have engaged with Landline content, opened emails, or visited the website) and 40% on targeted cold outreach to ideal customer profile (ICP) accounts.

**The 30-Day Rule.** The prospecting you do (or fail to do) in any 30-day window directly impacts your pipeline 90 days later. This creates urgency around daily prospecting discipline. For Landline reps, this translates to a non-negotiable daily calling block — typically 90 minutes to 2 hours — that is protected from meetings, admin, and other distractions.

**The Law of Familiarity.** The more prospects are familiar with you, your company, and your brand, the more likely they are to take your call and respond to your emails. This principle is why Landline's multi-channel approach (combining calls with email sequences, LinkedIn engagement, and content sharing) dramatically outperforms single-channel cold calling.

**The 5-Step Telephone Prospecting Framework:**

1. Get the prospect's attention by using their name
2. Identify yourself and your company
3. Tell them why you are calling (a brief, compelling reason tied to their world)
4. Build a bridge with a because statement that connects your reason to their likely situation
5. Ask for what you want — the meeting, the next step

**Application to Landline:** Every cold call should follow this 5-step structure. The "because" statement should reference a specific manufacturing pain point discovered through pre-call research — scrap rates, quality holds, compliance issues, or equipment downtime.

### 1.2 Smart Calling — Art Sobczak

Art Sobczak rejects the term "cold calling" entirely, arguing that no call should ever be truly cold. His methodology centers on pre-call intelligence gathering that transforms cold outreach into informed, warm conversations.

**The Pre-Call Research Framework.** Before picking up the phone, you "get smart" about the prospect. Sobczak prescribes researching prospects through LinkedIn profiles, company websites, press releases, trade journals, industry news, and smart Google searches. The goal is to find trigger events — promotions, expansions, new product launches, regulatory changes, equipment purchases, quality issues — that create natural openings for conversation.

**Social Engineering (Ethical Intelligence Gathering).** Sobczak introduces the concept of making preliminary calls to non-decision-makers within the target company to gather intelligence. This might mean calling the front desk, an engineer, or a line supervisor to learn about current challenges, who handles quality decisions, and what systems they currently use. For manufacturing targets, calling the plant floor during off-peak hours can yield surprisingly candid information about current pain points.

**The Possible Value Proposition (PVP).** Rather than leading with what your product does, Sobczak advocates opening with a possible value proposition — a hypothesis about what value you might bring, framed as a question. For Landline, this sounds like: "We've been working with injection molding companies to reduce their scrap rates by 15-30% using AI-powered quality monitoring. I'm not sure if that's something you're dealing with, but I wanted to see if it was worth a conversation."

**Key Principles for Landline Reps:**

- Never call blind. Spend 3-5 minutes researching each prospect before dialing.
- Have a clear, defined objective for every call (typically: secure a 15-minute discovery meeting).
- Position yourself as a problem solver, not a product pitcher.
- Use a consultative tone that respects the prospect's expertise and time.

### 1.3 Cold Calling Techniques — Stephan Schiffman

Schiffman's framework is built on a deceptively simple premise: the only goal of a cold call is to get an appointment. Everything else — qualifying, presenting, closing — happens later. This clarity of purpose eliminates the pressure to "sell" on the first call and dramatically improves conversion rates.

**The Ledge Technique.** The Ledge is Schiffman's signature tactic for turning objections into opportunities. When a prospect pushes back — "We're happy with our current system," "We don't have budget," "Send me some information" — the rep uses that statement as a "ledge" to pivot toward scheduling a meeting. The approach treats reservations not as endpoints but as doorways.

How it works in practice:

- Prospect: "We already have a quality management system."
- Rep: "That's actually exactly why I'm calling. A lot of the manufacturing companies we work with had existing systems too, but they found they were still spending 12-15 hours a week on manual quality checks. I'd love to show you how they cut that time in half. Would Thursday at 2 work for a quick 15-minute demo?"

The key is that the "ledge" shifts the conversation from whether to meet to when to meet.

**Question-Based Openers.** Schiffman advocates crafting opening statements that immediately engage the prospect by asking strategic questions rather than making statements. Instead of "I'm calling to tell you about our AI quality platform," the opener becomes: "I'm curious — how are you currently tracking quality deviations across your production lines?"

**The Appointment-Setting Formula:**

1. Open with a question that surfaces a relevant pain point
2. Listen actively for the "ledge" — any statement you can build upon
3. Connect the ledge to a specific result you've achieved for similar companies
4. Propose a specific time for a meeting (not "sometime next week" but "Thursday at 2:00")
5. Confirm the appointment with a calendar invite and brief agenda

### 1.4 New Sales. Simplified. — Mike Weinberg

Weinberg's framework is structured around three elements he calls the "New Sales Driver": building a strategic target list, crafting a compelling sales story, and selecting and deploying the right prospecting tools.

**Strategic Target List.** Weinberg insists that prospecting without a curated target list is like hunting without knowing what you're hunting. The target list should mirror your best existing customers — companies that are similar in size, industry, challenges, and buying behavior. For Landline's manufacturing focus, this means identifying companies by sub-sector (injection molding, CNC, stamping, food & beverage), revenue range ($10M-$500M), employee count (50-2000), and geography.

Building the list requires answering: Who are your 25 best current customers? What do they have in common? Where can you find more companies just like them?

**The Sales Story.** Your sales story is a compelling, customer-focused narrative that answers the question: "Tell me about your company." It is not a product feature dump. Weinberg's structure follows this pattern:

1. Open with the customer issues you address (not what you sell)
2. Describe the business outcomes you deliver
3. Share a brief proof point or customer example
4. Differentiate from the status quo (not competitors)

For Landline, the sales story might sound like: "We work with manufacturing companies that are frustrated by the gap between their quality goals and their actual defect rates. Our AI quality management platform typically helps them reduce scrap by 20-40%, catch defects 10x faster than manual inspection, and eliminate the fire-drill mentality that comes with quality holds. Companies like [customer example] went from a 6% scrap rate to under 2% within 90 days."

**Prospecting Weapons Arsenal.** Weinberg identifies several key tools (he calls them "weapons") that every sales rep should have ready to deploy: the sales story itself, a proactive phone call script, email templates, case studies, customer references, and a one-page leave-behind. Each weapon is designed for a specific stage and context in the prospecting process.

### 1.5 Combo Prospecting — Tony Hughes

Tony Hughes's framework recognizes that modern B2B buyers are harder to reach than ever, and no single channel is sufficient. Combo Prospecting orchestrates multiple channels into tightly sequenced "combos" that dramatically increase response rates.

**The Triple.** The foundational combo is the "Triple" — a phone call, voicemail, and email all completed within two minutes. The phone call attempts live contact. If it goes to voicemail, leave a brief message. Then immediately send a short email referencing the voicemail. This tight grouping ensures the prospect encounters your name through multiple channels in rapid succession.

**The Quad, Quint, and Sextet.** Building on the Triple, Hughes adds social media touches — a LinkedIn connection request or comment, a Twitter interaction, or a text message — to create more complex combos. A "Quad" adds a LinkedIn touch to the Triple. A "Quint" adds both LinkedIn and a text. The key is that all touches occur within a narrow window (ideally under 5 minutes), creating a "surround sound" effect.

**Point of View (POV) Messaging.** Hughes emphasizes that every touch in the combo must be grounded in a compelling point of view — a perspective on the prospect's business that demonstrates genuine understanding and insight. For manufacturing prospects, this might be a POV on how AI is transforming quality management in their specific sub-industry, or how their competitors are gaining an edge through predictive quality analytics.

**Application to Landline's Sequence Engine:** Landline's email sequence engine should be configured to trigger a "Triple" notification when key engagement signals occur — when a prospect opens an email multiple times, clicks a link, or visits the pricing page. The rep gets an alert: "Call [Prospect Name] now, then leave a voicemail and send a follow-up email within 2 minutes."

## Part 2: Optimal Call Timing for Manufacturing Companies

### Best Days to Call

Manufacturing operations run on predictable schedules, and calling at the right time dramatically impacts connect rates.

**Tuesday through Thursday** are the highest-performing days for reaching manufacturing decision-makers. Tuesday and Wednesday mornings are particularly effective because production schedules are established for the week and managers have completed Monday planning meetings.

**Monday** is the worst day for cold calling manufacturing — plant managers are dealing with weekend production issues, reviewing Monday morning reports, and managing crew scheduling. Connection rates drop 30-50% compared to mid-week.

**Friday after 2:00 PM** is also a dead zone. Manufacturing managers are focused on closing out the week, reviewing production numbers, and planning for Monday.

### Best Times of Day

**Early morning (7:30-9:00 AM local time):** Manufacturing executives and plant managers start early. Many are at their desks reviewing production reports before the floor gets busy. This is often the best window for reaching operations directors and VP-level contacts.

**Mid-morning (10:00-11:30 AM):** The second-best window. Morning meetings are wrapping up, and managers have a brief gap before lunch. Decision-makers are settled into their workday and more receptive to conversations.

**Late afternoon (4:00-5:00 PM):** The third-best window. The production day is winding down, and managers are transitioning from operational mode to strategic thinking. They're also more likely to be at their desks rather than on the floor.

**Dead zones to avoid:** 12:00-2:00 PM (lunch and early afternoon slump) and 2:00-3:30 PM (afternoon meetings and production reviews).

### Industry-Specific Timing Notes

**Injection molding and stamping plants** often run multiple shifts. Try calling between shifts (typically 6:00-7:00 AM, 2:00-3:00 PM, or 10:00-11:00 PM for third-shift managers) to catch supervisors during transition.

**Food and beverage plants** with USDA or FDA inspectors on-site may have specific windows when quality managers are available. Early morning (before inspectors arrive) and late afternoon (after inspections) tend to be best.

**CNC shops** with job-shop operations often have owners and managers who arrive early (6:30-7:30 AM) and stay late. The early morning window is often the most productive for reaching decision-makers at smaller CNC operations.

### Holiday and Seasonal Considerations

Avoid calling during plant shutdown periods, which are common in manufacturing: the week of July 4th, the last two weeks of December, and often the first week of January. Many plants also shut down for a week in the summer (varies by company). Connect rates drop 40-60% during these windows.

## Part 3: Gatekeeper Strategies

Gatekeepers in manufacturing — typically office managers, executive assistants, or front desk staff — serve an essential function and should be treated as allies, not obstacles.

### Strategy 1: The Ally Approach

Treat the gatekeeper as a knowledgeable insider who can help you reach the right person. Manufacturing companies often have complex organizational structures, and the gatekeeper may know who actually makes quality and technology decisions.

Script: "Hi, I'm hoping you can help me. I'm looking for the person who handles quality management decisions for the plant. Would that be [name from research], or is there someone else I should speak with?"

### Strategy 2: The Referral Request

Rather than asking to be put through, ask who the right person is. This positions you as a professional who values precision over persistence.

Script: "I work with manufacturing companies on reducing scrap rates through AI-powered quality management. I'm not sure who would be the right person to discuss this with — could you point me in the right direction?"

### Strategy 3: The Early/Late Call

Call before 8:00 AM or after 5:30 PM. In manufacturing environments, decision-makers often answer their own phones outside of normal business hours because administrative staff aren't present.

### Strategy 4: The Direct Dial Strategy

Use tools like ZoomInfo, Apollo, or LinkedIn Sales Navigator to find direct dial numbers for specific contacts. Manufacturing VP-level contacts often have direct lines that bypass the front desk entirely.

### Strategy 5: The Department Transfer

Call the sales department first (they almost always answer) and ask to be transferred to engineering, quality, or operations. Internal transfers often bypass gatekeeper screening.

### What to Avoid

Never lie to a gatekeeper about who you are or why you're calling. Manufacturing is a relationship-driven industry with tight-knit communities. Deception will burn bridges not just with one company but potentially across an entire regional manufacturing network.

## Part 4: Voicemail Scripts

In manufacturing B2B sales, voicemail is not a fallback — it is a strategic tool. Decision-makers in manufacturing listen to voicemails because they may signal supply chain issues, customer problems, or quality alerts. A well-crafted voicemail that sounds relevant will get a callback or at minimum prime the prospect for your follow-up email.

### Core Voicemail Principles

- Keep it under 30 seconds (20-25 seconds is ideal)
- Lead with your name and company only once, at the beginning
- State a specific, relevant reason for calling
- End with your phone number spoken slowly (repeat it)
- Leave voicemails as part of a Combo (follow immediately with email)

### Voicemail Script 1: The Pain Point Hook

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Landline. I'm reaching out because we've been helping [similar company type — e.g., injection molding companies] reduce their scrap rates by 20-30% using AI-powered quality monitoring. I wanted to see if that's a challenge you're dealing with as well. My number is [phone number]. Again, that's [phone number]. I'll also shoot you a quick email with some details."

### Voicemail Script 2: The Trigger Event

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Landline. I noticed [company] recently [trigger event — expanded production, opened a new line, received a new contract, had a quality recall]. We work with manufacturing companies going through exactly that kind of transition to make sure quality keeps up with growth. I'd love to share how. My number is [phone number] — that's [phone number]. I'll follow up by email as well."

### Voicemail Script 3: The Referral

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Landline. [Referral name] at [referral company] suggested I give you a call. We helped them [specific result — cut their quality hold time from 48 hours to 4 hours], and they thought you might be dealing with something similar. I'd love to chat for 10 minutes. My number is [phone number]. Again, [phone number]."

### Voicemail Script 4: The Follow-Up

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Landline — I sent you an email last week about how we're helping manufacturers catch quality issues before they become production shutdowns. I know you're busy, so I'll keep this quick: we have a 15-minute demo that shows exactly how it works for [their specific industry]. Would love to set that up whenever works for you. [Phone number]. Thanks, [Name]."

## Part 5: Objection Handling Frameworks

### The LAER Framework (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond)

This four-step framework ensures you never dismiss an objection or jump to a counter-argument prematurely.

**Listen** — Let the prospect finish their objection completely. Don't interrupt, even if you've heard it a thousand times.

**Acknowledge** — Validate their concern. "That makes sense" or "I hear that a lot from manufacturing leaders" shows respect and builds trust.

**Explore** — Ask a follow-up question to understand the real objection beneath the surface objection. "When you say you're happy with your current system, what specifically is working well for you?"

**Respond** — Only after you understand the real concern do you offer your response, ideally framed as a story about a similar company.

### Common Objections and Responses

**Objection 1: "We're happy with our current system."**

Acknowledge: "That's great to hear — it sounds like you've got a solid process in place."
Explore: "Just out of curiosity, how much time does your team spend on manual quality checks each week?"
Respond: "Most of the companies we work with felt the same way before they saw what AI-powered monitoring could add on top of their existing systems. [Company X] was using [current system] too, and they found they were still missing about 15% of defects that our system catches in real-time. Would it be worth 15 minutes to see how we complement what you already have?"

**Objection 2: "We don't have the budget."**

Acknowledge: "Budget is always a consideration, especially in manufacturing where margins matter."
Explore: "Can I ask — what does scrap and rework cost you annually? Most plants we talk to find it's between 3-8% of revenue."
Respond: "What we've found is that the platform typically pays for itself within 60-90 days through scrap reduction alone. [Company X] was spending $400K a year on quality-related waste and cut that by 40% in the first quarter. Would it make sense to look at the ROI numbers together?"

**Objection 3: "Send me some information."**

Acknowledge: "Absolutely, I'd be happy to."
Explore: "So I can send you the most relevant case study, can I ask — what's the biggest quality challenge you're dealing with right now?"
Respond: "Perfect, I'll send over a case study from a [similar industry] company that was dealing with exactly that. After you've had a chance to review it, would [specific day] work for a quick 10-minute follow-up call to answer any questions?"

**Objection 4: "I'm too busy right now."**

Acknowledge: "I completely understand — I know how demanding manufacturing operations are."
Respond: "I respect your time, which is why our demo is only 15 minutes. And honestly, most of our customers tell us the platform saves them 5-10 hours a week on quality management. Would early next week work better — say Tuesday at 7:30 AM before things get hectic?"

**Objection 5: "We tried AI/technology before and it didn't work."**

Acknowledge: "I hear that more than you'd think, and honestly, a lot of early AI tools for manufacturing weren't ready for prime time."
Explore: "What did you try, and what specifically didn't work?"
Respond: "That's really helpful context. The reason we built Landline specifically for manufacturing — not as a generic AI tool adapted to manufacturing — is exactly because of stories like yours. Our system is trained on manufacturing-specific data, and it integrates directly with your existing equipment and processes. [Company X] had a similar experience with [previous vendor] and found that our approach was fundamentally different. Would you be open to a quick demo so you can judge for yourself?"

**Objection 6: "I need to talk to my [boss/partner/team]."**

Acknowledge: "Of course — a decision like this should involve the right people."
Respond: "What if we set up a brief meeting that includes them? That way, everyone can see the demo and ask questions at the same time, and I can make sure to address the specific concerns they'd have. Who else would need to be part of the conversation?"

**Objection 7: "Your competitor offers something similar for less."**

Acknowledge: "Price is definitely important, and I appreciate you being upfront about that."
Explore: "Which platform are you comparing us to? I want to make sure we're looking at apples to apples."
Respond: "The key difference most of our manufacturing customers highlight is [specific differentiator — detection accuracy, ease of integration, time to value, manufacturing-specific training data]. [Company X] actually switched to us from [competitor] because [specific reason]. Would it help to see a side-by-side comparison?"

## Part 6: Combining Cold Calls with Email Sequences

### The Integrated Cadence Model

Cold calls should never exist in isolation. They are most effective when embedded within a multi-channel cadence that combines email, phone, voicemail, LinkedIn, and (for high-value prospects) direct mail.

### Recommended 21-Day Manufacturing Cadence

**Day 1 — Email #1: The Insight Email**
Subject: "[Company Name] — a question about your quality process"
Content: Brief, personalized email referencing a specific observation about their company and asking a thought-provoking question about their quality management approach.

**Day 2 — Phone Call #1 + Voicemail + Follow-up Email**
Execute a "Triple" (Tony Hughes): Call the prospect, leave a voicemail if no answer, and immediately send a brief email referencing the voicemail. The email subject line: "Just left you a voicemail."

**Day 4 — LinkedIn Connection Request**
Send a personalized connection request referencing the email. Keep it to 2-3 sentences. Do not pitch in the connection request.

**Day 6 — Email #2: The Case Study Email**
Share a relevant case study from a company in the same manufacturing sub-sector. Focus on specific, quantified results (scrap reduction percentage, time saved, cost impact).

**Day 8 — Phone Call #2 + Voicemail**
Reference the case study email. Use the trigger event voicemail script if applicable.

**Day 10 — LinkedIn Engagement**
Like or comment on one of the prospect's recent LinkedIn posts. Share a relevant industry article and tag them if appropriate.

**Day 12 — Email #3: The Value-Add Email**
Share a piece of useful content — an industry report, a relevant article, a benchmark statistic — with no ask. Position yourself as a resource, not just a salesperson.

**Day 15 — Phone Call #3 + Voicemail + Email**
Another "Triple." Reference previous touches. The voicemail should introduce a new angle or piece of information.

**Day 17 — LinkedIn Direct Message**
Send a short, conversational message. Reference a shared connection, a piece of their content, or a relevant industry event.

**Day 19 — Email #4: The Breakup Email**
Subject: "Should I close your file?"
Content: A brief, respectful email acknowledging that the timing may not be right and offering to reconnect in the future. This email consistently generates the highest response rates in outbound sequences because it triggers loss aversion.

**Day 21 — Final Phone Call**
Last attempt. Reference the breakup email. If no answer, leave a final voicemail and move the prospect to a long-term nurture sequence with monthly touches.

### Cadence Optimization Rules

- If the prospect opens an email more than 3 times, immediately trigger a phone call (regardless of where you are in the cadence)
- If the prospect clicks a link in an email, move up the next phone call by 1 day
- If the prospect replies to any email (even a "not interested"), switch to manual, personalized follow-up
- If the prospect connects on LinkedIn, send a thank-you message and move up the next phone call
- If you get a live conversation at any point, reset the cadence based on the outcome of the call

### Tracking and Measurement

For each cadence, track the following metrics in Landline:

- Connection rate (live conversations / total calls)
- Voicemail-to-callback rate
- Email open rate and click rate by position in sequence
- LinkedIn acceptance rate
- Meeting set rate (meetings / total prospects entered into cadence)
- Meeting show rate
- Pipeline generated per cadence
- Revenue closed per cadence

These metrics should be reviewed weekly to optimize timing, messaging, and channel mix.

## Part 7: Key Metrics and Activity Standards

### Daily Activity Targets for Landline Reps

Based on the frameworks above and calibrated for manufacturing B2B sales with a mid-market average deal size:

- 40-60 outbound dials per day
- 8-12 live conversations per day (15-20% connect rate target)
- 15-25 personalized emails per day
- 5-10 LinkedIn touches per day
- 2-3 meetings set per day (target)

### Weekly Pipeline Goals

- 10-15 new meetings set per week
- 8-12 meetings held per week (accounting for no-shows and cancellations)
- 3-5 qualified opportunities advanced per week
- 1-2 proposals or demos delivered per week

### Monthly Outcome Targets

- 40-50 meetings held per month
- 15-20 qualified opportunities in pipeline
- 5-8 proposals delivered
- 2-4 deals closed

## Appendix: Quick Reference Card

### The Perfect Manufacturing Cold Call (Under 2 Minutes)

1. **Open (10 seconds):** "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Landline. Did I catch you at an okay time?"
2. **Reason for calling (15 seconds):** "The reason I'm calling is that we've been working with [similar companies] to [specific outcome — reduce scrap rates / catch defects earlier / eliminate quality holds]."
3. **Bridge question (10 seconds):** "I'm curious — is that something your team is dealing with?"
4. **Listen and respond (30-60 seconds):** Use the LAER framework for any objection. Find the "ledge" (Schiffman).
5. **Ask for the meeting (15 seconds):** "I'd love to show you a quick 15-minute demo of how it works. Would [specific day and time] work for you?"
6. **Confirm and close (10 seconds):** "Great, I'll send you a calendar invite with a link. Looking forward to it."

### Pre-Call Checklist (Sobczak Method)

Before every call, confirm you know:

- [ ] Prospect's name, title, and role
- [ ] Company name, size, and manufacturing sub-sector
- [ ] Recent news, trigger events, or company announcements
- [ ] Current quality management approach (if discoverable)
- [ ] A specific, relevant pain point to reference
- [ ] Your opening question
- [ ] Your Possible Value Proposition (PVP)
- [ ] Your specific ask (meeting time and date)
